# Resuscitation

## ALS

Good sim [here](https://youtu.be/jQYHQr3ebLo?si=M4BQOxAIbm7u8XvE)

**My learning points**

- Consider CALS

- Team role allocation

  - TL

  - Airway

  - CPR 1 and 2

  - Defib

  - Drugs

  - Echo

  - Scribe

- Definitively assess A, B, C and don’t move on before fully assessed

- Key focus: CPR quality & consistency, efficiency of shock

- ==Consider double sequential* shock if refractory VT==

  - ==As close to simultaneous* as possible==

  - ==Risks frying the defib, not in guidelines but supported by DOSE-VF trial (although significant methodological limitations)==

- Call for echo

- ==After each shock, resume CPR immediately without a rhythm check; perform rhythm and pulse checks only during the scheduled 2-min pauses, unless there are obvious signs of ROSC.==

- ==If art line in situ, aim for dBP >35==

  - ==this is the whole point of the adrenaline, to support a diastolic/coronary perfusion pressure==

  - ==omit/increase/decrease the adrenaline dose to achieve this==

  - ==remember to check the dBP visually during CPR (it’s point B, not point A)==

  - ![](papers/revision-media/image1.png)

- ==Ask for mechanical CPR – no evidence that its better than hand CPR but its equivalent and it quietens the room, allocate someone to monitor its position==

- ==Use app to guide timing, record events and use metronome==

- ==Lignocaine dose = 100mg (10ml of 1% or 5ml of 2%)==

- ==If family are in the hospital, consider offering them the opportunity to be present==

  - Data is poor but generally supportive, at least of having been offered

  - My practice: if feeling is approaching futility offer family to come in to see the last bit (gives them the benefit but minimal risk to compromising quality of earlier resus efforts)

**Drugs**

- Adrenaline - 1mg every 4mins/2nd cycle

- Amiodarone 300mg after 3rd shock, 150mg after 5th shock

- NaHCO3 (>15mins arrest, TCA, metabolic acidosis) - 50-100mls 8.4% NaHCO3

  - ==evidence shows bicarbonate is associated with worse neurological outcomes==

- TdP – 20mmol MgSO4

- PE – alteplase 50mg bolus, consider further 50 mg after 15 mins; continue CPR for 60-90mins

- Toxins

  - Cyanide toxicity - hydroxycobalamin 5mg, repeat up to 15mg

  - TCA – 100ml 8.4% NaHCO3

  - Local anaesthetic toxicity - 1.5ml/kg of 20% intralipid followed by infusion and further boluses per protocol

  - Digoxin - Digibind + MgSO4

  - Organophosphates/nerve agents - atropine

  - Other toxins - antidotes not proven to work in arrest scenario but reasonable to attempt, eg naloxone

**Reversible causes**

- ↑/↓ temp

- ↑/↓ K

- ↓ BSL

- Hypoxia

- Toxins inc anaphylaxis

- PE/AMI

- Tamponade

- Tension pneumothorax and dynamic hyperinflation

### CPR induced consciousness

- Approx 1% of resus

- Good prognostic factor

- Very limited research

- No global consensus on management, most guidelines suggest ketamine sedation

### EtCO2 during arrest

- Generally a reliable indicator of cardiac output during CPR

- >10-20 suggests good CPR

- >20 mmHg at 20 minutes CPR -> higher chance of ROSC

- <10 mmHg at 20 minutes CPR -> almost no chance of ROSC 

### When to stop

Multimodal assessment. Poor prognostic factors:

- Asystole >20mins without reversible cause

- EtCO2 <10 after 20mins CPR

- No cardiac activity on echo

- Initial non-shockable rhythm

- >1hr of resus (if no ECMO), >90 mins (even if ECMO)

Observational data tells us that:

- Each additional minute of CPR reduces odds of survival to hospital discharge by 13%

- Even for IHCA, after 20 minutes of CPR the odds of survival with favourable neurological outcome are 8% for young/witnessed/shockable arrests

- By 45mins of arrest chance of survival essentially zero

**Long‑term outcomes after IHCA (≥3 months)**

- Poor neuro outcome (survivors): ~10%

  - This is pretty blood good! If you survive 90% are have a good neuro outcome!!

- Anxiety/depression: ~13% / ~8%.

- HRQoL impairments common: pain/discomfort ~61%, usual activities ~53%, anxiety/depression ~49%.

## Transcutaneous pacing

- **ECG leads on!**

- Pads in A-P position

- Sync mode on defib

- Start on lowest energy and titrate up until reliable electrical and mechanical capture (usually 50-100mA). Then set energy to 10mA above minimum threshold

Altered management until day 10 post op

 

- ==Can delay CPR for up to 60secs to assess rhythm and troubleshoot==

  - ==VF/VT - 3 stacked shocks, if unsuccessful immediate amiodarone 300mg via CVC==

  - ==PEA - stop pacing to exclude underlying VF==

- ==Amiodarone and atropine are the only drugs in the protocol==

  - ==Stop infusions, if adrenaline considered give 50mcg boluses==

- ==Prepare for resternotomy immediately (call surgeons and extra assistance), commence procedure if no ROSC after 4 mins==

- ==Other steps for resus in an ICU patient==

  - ==Disconnect ventilator, BVM with 100% O2 and no PEEP==

  - ==If IABP switch to pressure trigger==

 

==Otherwise as for ALS, including assessment for Hs and Ts.==

 

==Procedure for resternotomy==

- ==Establish team leader and delegate roles - ALS team and resternotomy team==

- ==2 proceduralists gown and glove (no handwash), setup tray==

- ==Non-sterile team stop CPR and remove sternotomy dressing==

- ==Proceduralists drop iodine onto chest then apply sterile drape==

- ==Proceduralists restart external CPR (sterile fashion)==

- ==Scalpel to open incision to sternal wires==

- ==Cut sternal wires w wire cutters and remove them with heavy needle holder==

- ==Suction out clot==

**Emergency management of brainstem herniation**

- Drain CSF if EVD present

- Hyperventilate to 25-30mmHg

- Mannitol 1g/kg IV stat (ie 200-350ml of 20% mannitol)

- Thiopentone 250-500mg IV stat

- Support sBP >110

### Acute spinal resus

As with TBI, there is no treatment to alleviate/reverse the primary injury, the goal is to prevent secondary injury. The aim to recover at least one motor level over time, eg C3 -> C4 may get off ventilator, C5 -> C6 may be able to drive own wheelchair.

## Massive transfusion 

- ==Reverse any anticoagulants==

- ==If on antiplatelet consider transfusion or desmopressin==

  - ==NB: Do not give platelets for this indication in ICH, ↑ mortality in PATCH==

- ==Tranexamic acid==

  - 1g over 10minutes, ideally given <3h of injury, then 1g over 8 hours (CRASH-2)

- Keep T>35° by avoiding unnecessary exposure + blood/fluid warmers +/- Bair Hugger

- Balanced resuscitation

  - Activate MTP, enlisting support of lab and haematologist

  - Start with 1:1:1* RBC:FFP:Plt

    - NOTE THIS IS ~4:4:1 bags

    - FFP = 15ml/kg/dose (~3-4 bags, takes 20mins to thaw)

    - One bag of platelets is pooled from 4 donors

  - Repeatedly check TEG/ROTEM/coags/iCa/pH and tailor products aiming

    - iCa >1.1

    - Plt >50 unless TBI then >100

    - Fibrinogen >1 (>2 in obstetrics)

    - APTT <40, INR <1.5

  - Replace fibrinogen with cryoprecipitate

    - 4g/dose (10 standard or 5 apheresis units; 20-30mins to thaw)

  - If ongoing catastrophic and uncontrollable bleeding despite normal coags consider recombinant Factor VIIa (Novoseven)

- In the absence of TBI, target resuscitation to clinical indicators of tissue perfusion and lactate rather than strict sBP targets

*There is still ambiguity about optimal ratio of blood products in MTP

- PROPPR (2015) – Landmark RCT, 680 adult trauma pts needing MTP. 1:1:1 vs 2:1:1. No overall difference in mortality at 24h or 30d, but mortality from exsanguination specifically was improved.

- Observational studies all likely compromised by survivor bias (survivors alive long enough to receive more products)

## Laryngoscopy

![](papers/revision-media/image10.jpeg)Laryngoscopy

- Position yourself

  - Patient’s airway should be no higher than your umbilicus

  - Stand straight with elbow tucked on ASIS and bent at 90deg. Then bend elbow further 15deg and push out from body at 15deg

  - Hold the bag in your right hand against your upper chest

  - Lean back

- Optimise patient position

  - Extend neck (BUT not too much or you can cause 'epiglottis camouflage', where the epiglottis edge disappears against the pharyngeal mucosa)

  - Open mouth (ask assistant to also hold mouth open)

- Hold blade with left hand very low/close to the blade (it’s a battery holder not a handle)

- Insert blade

  - Hold gently with battery holder pointed to patient’s feet

  - Insert 3cm into right hand side* of mouth

    - If battery holder hits chest point it to the right until you’re in and then rotate – like [this](https://youtu.be/x2FOdgw93sA?t=165)

  - Sweep the tongue gently to the left

  - Advance slowly, **tilting** the blade identifying uvula, epiglottis and vallecula

  - Once the tip is in the vallecula, engage the hypoepiglottic ligament and then **lift** the blade towards to roof line across the room

- Identify glottic structures

  - the first glottic structures seen are the posterior cartilages (arytenoids) and interarytenoid notch, before the glottic opening and the vocal cords

  - If poor/no view use BURP

  - If poor/no view use your right hand to lift the head off the pillow

  - Third line move is to do two handed laryngoscopy, find the view, then release right hand once you’ve locked your left elbow against your chest – see [here](https://youtu.be/ccI6PAmXNxM?t=81)

- Insert ETT **horizontally** (like [this](https://youtu.be/x2FOdgw93sA?t=458))

  - always keep the tube tip below the line of vision and watch it travel upward to the larynx

  - ensure that the tip passes anterior to the interarytenoid notch

  - If you get hung up rotate the tube to left (**anticlockwise**)

## Trache emergency

- Code blue

- 100% O2 via face + tracheostomy

- Assess patency

  - Remove speaking valve and inner cannula

  - Pass suction catheter -> if not possible the trache is not patent -> deflate the cuff

  - Look/listen/feel at mouth -> if not achieving ventilation remove the trache

  - Look for subcut emphysema, obvious migration, loss of EtCO2

  - Plan D: ECMO

# Respiratory

## Hypoxia 

**DDx for acute desaturation on ventilator:** DOPES

- Disconnection

- Obstruction

- Patient

- Equipment problem

- Stacking

**General management**

- Treat cause (eg: antibiotics for pneumonia)

- Monitor for and treat reversible complications like pneumothorax, pleural effusions, fluid overload

- Supplemental O2

  - Aim SpO2 92-96% unless chronically hypercapnic (no harm from conservative O2 approach and avoids risks of hyperoxia (LOCO2, ICU-ROX, HOT-ICU, Oxygen-ICU)

  - Lung protective ventilation

- Chest physiotherapy, suctioning

- Bronchoscopy – suction secretions and exclude mucus plugging

- Minimise respiratory demand (treat acidosis, pain, fever)

- Diurese to euvolaemia

- Optimise O2 delivery (Hb and cardiac output)

Rescue measures

- Heavy sedation

- [Neuromuscular blockade](#_heading=h.1y810tw) (minimize O2 consumption and CO2 production)

  - ACURASYS (2010) – patients with mod-severe ARDS, 48h of NMB early in dz course, found mortality benefit

  - ROSE (2019) – Larger sample than ACURASYS. Control group had lighter sedation and used paralysis only on “as needed” basis, which was about 15%. Found no mortality benefit to routine NMB

- Proning, ideally 16h/day (PROSEVA demonstrated mortality benefit)

- Inhaled pulmonary dilators (no mortality benefit)

- VV-ECMO

  - CESAR found mortality benefit in ARDS but significant methodological failings

  - EOLIA found no mortality benefit but 30% of control group ended up on ECMO

- E-COR

  - REST (2021) – 412 patients with mod-severe ARDS, <48h hypoxaemia. ECOR with Vt 3ml/kg IBW vs ARDSnet ventilation. No mortality benefit at 90d, trial stopped early due to futility. Weaknesses:

    - likely underpowered

    - many sites unfamiliar with ECOR despite known volume-outcome relationship with ECLS

    - unblinded study

    - only 6% screened patients were randomised (possible selection bias)

## High airway pressure

DDx for acute, reversible causes of high airway pressures = Deep Sedation, Muscle Paralysis and Adrenaline

- ==Dyssynchrony==

- Spasm (bronchospasm)

- ==Mucus plug==

- Pneumothorax

- Anaphylaxis

Management

- FiO2 100%

- Quickly assess and confirm:

  - airway patency (EtCO2, ability to pass suction catheter)

  - ventilator function (pressures, volume, flow-time curve)

  - haemodynamic stability

- Switch to bag ventilation, manually assess compliance and determine if problem is vent/circuit or tube/patient

  - Vent/circuit problem: condensation in circuit, clogged HME, ventilator settings or malfunction

  - Tube/patient problem: blocked, dislodged, migrated, cuff leak or small tube, or patient deterioration (eg: bronchospasm, pulmonary oedema, PE, pneumothorax etc)

- Assess patient

  - ABG

  - POCUS ?lung sliding ?APO

  - CXR

  - ECG

  - Consider bronchoscopy

- Scrutinise ventilator for evidence of dyssynchrony (cf dyssynchrony section)

- Treat cause once identified

## Wolf-Parkinson-White

Baseline ECG

- Short (<120ms) PR

- Delta wave (causing wide [>100ms] QRS)

  - Negatively deflected delta waves in anterior/inferior leads can cause pseudo-Q waves (shown below in II and aVF)

- ==Discordant ST-segment and T-wave changes==

![](papers/revision-media/image26.png)

ECG in acute presentations

- Narrow complex (orthodromic) SVT

- Wide complex (antidromic) SVT

- AF/Aflutter

  - ==HR >200 should prompt consideration of pre-excitation syndrome (too fast to be going through AV node)==

  - Irregularly irregular

  - ==Subtle beat-to-beat variation in QRS morphology==

  - Axis remains stable, unlike Polymorphic VT

- VT / VF

  - This can happen especially if pt goes into AF/flutter and accessory pathway conducts 1:1 -> causes ventricular systole with rate 200-300

![](papers/revision-media/image27.jpg)

![](papers/revision-media/image28.jpg)

Management

- Any haemodynamically unstable tachyarrhythmia -> DCR

- Orthodromic SVT – may be safe to block the AV node in order to terminate the re-entrant loop

  - Vagal manoeuvre

  - Adenosine/beta-blocker/verapamil

- Antidromic SVT

  - The AV node may be stopping the conduction of some atrial action potentials.

  - If the underlying rhythm is AF, blocking the AV node (with adenosine, BB or CCB) could allow 1:1 conduction of the insanely high atrial depolarisation rate down the accessory pathway and precipitate VT/VF

  - THUS, the treatment is DCR or ==procainamide== (Class I antiarrythmic)

- AF with pre-excitation – as for antidromic SVT

- Long term treatment options include ablation, ==flecainide or amiodarone==

## VT

### Identification

Distinguish b/w VT and SVT w aberrancy.

Aberrancy = cause for wide QRS other than ventricular pacemaker (eg: BBB, accessory pathway, drugs, electrolytes).

Features c/w VT

- Regular

- QRS >160ms

- Concordance of chest leads (all + or all -)

- Extreme axis deviation (aVR +, aVF and I-)

- Capture / fusion beats

  - Narrow/near normal looking beats within a run of wide complexes

  - If completely normal/sinus beat = capture beat

  - If slightly wide/inverted/abnormal = fusion beat of supra and ventricular origin

- A-V dissociation

- ==QS complex in V6==

- ==RBBB morphology with taller left rabbit ear in V1==

- Simplified Brugada criterion: R wave peak time ≥50ms in lead II (one small square is 40ms)

Features suggestive SVT

- Known aberrant conduction (eg BBB)

- Known Wolf-Parkinson-White/known pre-excitation syndrome

Differential diagnosis for broad complex tachycardia

- ==VT==

- ==SVT/AF with BBB==

## Severe crashing pulmonary oedema (SCAPE) basic management checklist

## Hypertensive emergency – initial management

- Lower BP gradually, ~10-20% in first hour, then a further 5-15% in next 23h, UNLESS

## Gas embolism

**Immediate management**

- Stop source of air

- Left lateral position, head down (keep bubble in heart, away from RV outflow tract and away from brain)

- 100% O2 (de-nitrogenate)

- PEEP (although caution that high ITP might increase risk of shunting the bubble to systemic circulation)

- Aspirate central access

- HBOT to squish the bubble

- Antiepileptic Rx if cerebral gas embolism

- If aeromedical retrieval required need to minimise altitude. Hypobaric conditions of altitude will promote further gas to come out of solution

# Neurology

## Intracranial hypertension

- Head up at 30°, neck straight, avoid neck compression from C-collar, ETT ties, IJ lines

- PaCO2 35-40, avoid excess PEEP

- Avoid fever

- Bolus/↑ analgesia/sedation. Trial NMB

- Drain CSF @ 10cmH20 (monitor ICP q30mins)

- Osmotherapy

- Continuous EVD drainage

- Thiopentone bolus (250mg) +/- infusion with cEEG for burst suppression

- Consider MAP challenge

- Decompressive craniectomy

![](papers/revision-media/image34.png)

**Emergency management of brainstem herniation**

- Drain CSF if EVD present

- ==Hyperventilate to 25-30mmHg==

- Mannitol 1g/kg IV stat (ie 200-350ml of 20% mannitol)

- Thiopentone 250-500mg IV stat

- Support sBP >110

**The only measure not included in this flowchart is a MAP challenge**

- Record MAP, ICP and CPP

- Titrate vasopressor to ↑ MAP by 10mmHg for up to 20mins

- Observe effect on ICP and CPP

  - If ICP ↓ this suggests that cerebral pressure autoregulation is in-tact and you can consider leaving the patient at that higher MAP

![](papers/revision-media/image35.png)

***If needing high doses of vasopressor to achieve CPP**

If excessive vasopressor doses are required, continuing to aggressively pursue a CPP target of 60 mmHg may drive cerebral swelling and/or cause ischaemia in other organs. Options include considering:

- lowering the CPP target (in discussion with the neurosurgeon)

- ceasing ICP/CPP based therapy after 5 days

- hydrocortisone 200 mg IV, then 50 mg IV QID (after taking a serum cortisol level)

## Neonatal resus

RCH videos [here](https://www.neoresus.org.au/learning-resources/video-resources/)

Resuscitair

- Heater on

- Suction to 80mmHg

- IPPV set to air only (if start CPR change to FiO2 100%)

- PEEP to 5cmH20

- PIP to 30cmH20

Mx

- Birth -> dry, stimulate, keep warm

- If not breathing/blue/floppy assess breathing and HR

- HR <100/min -> IPPV with air at 60 breaths/min

- HR < 60/min and inadequate breathing

  - CPR (3:1)

  - IPPV with 100% O2 -> intubation

  - Cannulate umbilical vein (or IO, or ETT)

  - 20ml/kg 0.9% NaCl bolus + Adrenaline

*Avoid hyperoxia, use target SpO2 table on Resuscitair

## Paediatric ALS

**Differences from adult**

Peri-arrest

- Most common causes of arrest = hypoxia, hypotension, acidosis

- For haemodynamically unstable arrythmias the drugs are similar except no verapamil (severe cardiac depression)

- For infants, start CPR if HR <60

ALS

- Smaller face -> provide mouth-to-mouth to mouth and nose

- Greater emphasis of breaths

  - Rescue breaths before starting CPR

  - use 15:2 ratio compressions:breaths

- For infants use two thumbs (hands wrapped around torso) for CPR

- Paediatric pads for kids <10kg

- DCR energy 4J/kg

- Broselow tape for drug dosing

- In infants it’s okay to cannulate scalp veins

## Post partum haemorrhage

**Definition**

Controversial. Blood loss > 500 ml after a delivery (or > 250 ml after an abortion). Is not always brisk.

**Causes – the 4Ts**

1.  Trauma - uterine rupture, cervical tear, vaginal tear, perineal tear

2.  aTony – most common

3.  Tissue – retained placenta or membranes

4.  Thrombin – coagulation disorder or DIC

If patient is shocked but PV loss unremarkable consider intraabdominal haemorrhage from ruptured uterus, liver or spleen.

**Exam**

- Vitals

- Is the placenta intact? Any suspicion for retained products?

- Uterine atony as evidenced by palpable ‘boggy’ uterus in abdomen

- Vaginal exam – any lacerations or uterine inversion into vaginal vault?

- Any evidence of DIC?

*Simultaneous assessment and management*

## Amniotic fluid embolism

Clinical features

- Sudden deterioration during labour or immediately postpartum

- Haemodynamic collapse

  - Shock is initially obstructive, then evolves into distributive

  - Pathophysiologically a mix of PE and anaphylaxis physiology

  - Right heart failure initially -> LV failure later

- APO / ARDS

- DIC

- Nausea, vomiting

- Agitation, coma, seizures

Differential diagnosis

- PE / air embolism

- Haemorrhage

- Eclampsia

- Anaphylaxis

- Transfusion reaction

Management (all supportive)

- Emergent delivery

- CPR

- Left uterine displacement

- IVF, vasopressor

- Manage coagulopathy

## Perimortem caesarean

- Perform in situ, do not delay for transfer to OT

- ==Continue CPR throughout==

- ==Vertical incision from xiphoid to pubis.== Layers: skin, subcut tissue, fascia between the rectus muscles, peritoneum.

- ==Identify uterus - vertical incision through lower 1/3 uterus. Then cut the rest of the way with scissors taking care not to cut foetus.==

- ==Deliver baby, clamp and cut cord, start resus==

- ==Pack the open abdomen==
